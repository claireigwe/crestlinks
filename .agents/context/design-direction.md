# Design Direction

## Purpose

This document defines the visual and interaction direction for the CrestLinks website.

It is intended to guide design decisions throughout the project and ensure every page communicates the same premium, trustworthy, and professional experience.

This document does not define the design system. Colour palettes, typography, spacing, components, and design tokens are maintained separately.

---

# Design Philosophy

The CrestLinks website should present the company as a premium international agricultural export partner.

Rather than looking like a traditional farming website or a generic corporate business site, the experience should balance the warmth of agriculture with the professionalism of global trade.

The design should immediately communicate trust, quality, and confidence.

Visitors should feel they are engaging with an established business capable of serving international markets.

---

# Design Keywords

Every design decision should support one or more of these characteristics.

- Premium
- Modern
- Editorial
- Bright
- Professional
- Spacious
- Confident
- Authentic
- Timeless
- Human

If a design decision conflicts with these qualities, it should be reconsidered.

---

# Visual Experience

The website should feel open and inviting.

Layouts should prioritise whitespace, strong typography, and high-quality imagery over excessive decoration.

Content should breathe.

Every section should feel intentional rather than crowded.

The interface should encourage exploration without overwhelming visitors.

---

# Layout Direction

The website should use modern editorial layouts rather than repetitive marketing sections.

Layouts should alternate naturally throughout the page to create visual rhythm.

Examples include:

- Full-width hero sections.
- Split-content layouts.
- Large image sections.
- Card-based content grids.
- Horizontal product showcases.
- Process timelines.
- Statistics sections.
- Large call-to-action blocks.

Avoid repeating identical layouts throughout the page.

Every section should introduce a subtle variation while maintaining consistency.

---

# Hero Direction

The hero should immediately establish credibility and scale.

Requirements:

- Full-width layout.
- Bright, immersive photography.
- Clear value proposition.
- Strong primary call-to-action.
- Supporting secondary action.
- Minimal supporting interface elements.

The hero should feel cinematic without becoming distracting.

The imagery should communicate possibility, growth, and professionalism.

---

# Photography Direction

Photography is one of the strongest trust-building tools on the website.

Images should feel:

- Bright
- Natural
- Authentic
- High quality
- Professionally composed

Preferred subjects include:

- Agricultural landscapes
- Farmers
- Product harvesting
- Commodity close-ups
- Warehousing
- Packaging
- Shipping containers
- Port operations
- Export logistics

Photography should tell the story of the export journey rather than simply decorate the page.

Placeholder imagery may be used during development and replaced later with custom AI-generated or commissioned photography.

---

# Visual Hierarchy

The interface should rely primarily on:

- Typography
- Photography
- Whitespace
- Grid structure
- Scale

Visual hierarchy should not depend on excessive colour or decoration.

Every page should remain easy to scan.

---

# Component Philosophy

Components should feel refined rather than decorative.

Cards, buttons, forms, and navigation should appear lightweight, modern, and approachable.

Components should never compete with the content.

Instead, they should quietly support the user's journey.

---

# Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should always reinforce understanding.

Animation should communicate meaning rather than exist for visual entertainment.

Every interaction should answer one question:

**"Does this help the user understand, navigate, or interact with the interface more effectively?"**

If the answer is no, the interaction should not exist.

---

# Motion Principles

Motion should be:

- Subtle
- Purposeful
- Responsive
- Smooth
- Consistent

Animations should never slow users down.

The interface should feel responsive rather than animated.

---

# Recommended Interactions

Suitable interactions include:

### Hero

- Gentle entrance animations.
- Subtle background image scaling.
- Soft fade transitions.

---

### Navigation

- Smooth transition between transparent and solid navigation.
- Active navigation indicators.
- Refined hover states.

---

### Buttons

- Soft hover elevation.
- Directional icon movement.
- Responsive press states.

---

### Product Cards

- Gentle lift on hover.
- Image zoom.
- Clear interactive feedback.

---

### Statistics

- Count-up animations triggered once.
- Never repeat continuously.

---

### Timeline

- Progressive activation while scrolling.

---

### Images

- Very subtle parallax where appropriate.

---

### Forms

- Smooth focus states.
- Helpful validation feedback.
- Clear success states.

---

# Animation Principles

Avoid excessive movement.

Animations should:

- Support usability.
- Guide attention.
- Reinforce hierarchy.
- Feel polished.

Avoid:

- Large page transitions.
- Continuous looping animations.
- Decorative motion.
- Distracting hover effects.

---

# Section Rhythm

Every section should feel visually distinct while remaining part of the same system.

Avoid repeating identical layouts.

Instead, alternate between:

- Text-first.
- Image-first.
- Cards.
- Full-width imagery.
- Statistics.
- Timelines.
- Split layouts.
- Large CTAs.

This creates momentum throughout the page.

---

# Content Density

Content should remain concise.

Sections should communicate one primary idea.

Avoid combining multiple messages into a single section.

Visitors should always understand the purpose of the section within a few seconds.

---

# Imagery Strategy

Images should communicate:

- Scale.
- Professionalism.
- Freshness.
- Quality.
- Global trade.
- Partnership.

Avoid generic stock imagery that lacks authenticity.

Every image should strengthen buyer confidence.

---

# Accessibility

The experience should remain accessible across devices and input methods.

The design should support:

- Keyboard navigation.
- Screen readers.
- Colour contrast requirements.
- Clear focus indicators.
- Responsive layouts.

Accessibility should be considered a core design principle rather than a post-development task.

---

# Overall Experience

When an international buyer visits the CrestLinks website, they should immediately feel that they are interacting with a professional export company that understands international business expectations.

The experience should feel calm, confident, and trustworthy.

Rather than trying to impress visitors with visual complexity, the website should earn confidence through clarity, thoughtful interactions, strong photography, and disciplined design.

Every page should reinforce CrestLinks' position as a premium agricultural export partner.
